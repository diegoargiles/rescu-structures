# P07 wing-arm integration candidate

This revision develops the local wing connection around the P06 carrier. It preserves P04/P06 source files and motor geometry. It is a geometric and mass-accounting candidate, not a released aircraft joint design.

## Geometry evidence

The wing surfaces, tapered spar axis/cone, installed arm positions and inherited rib/saddle positions come from saved native CAD. Every new thickness, span, bond width and clearance is a design assumption listed in `data/inputs_p07.json`. A valid solid is not evidence of an achievable laminate thickness, adhesive strength or fatigue life.

The wing is thin around the spar. A closed sleeve was considered but not adopted as the default: it would consume the limited crown clearance and require a further skin/spar change. The modeled alternative uses two open side cheeks, bonded along the fore and aft sides of the existing tapered spar. It leaves the spar crown and lower surface open. The cheek shape follows the actual conical outside surface, not a nominal straight tube. Both sides of the aircraft use their own native geometry; the model is not generated by assuming perfect left/right symmetry.

The revised spar relief separates each original local rib into forward and aft web segments. Those are separate physical pieces with separate accounting. They are coupled through the spar connection and skin attachments; there is no assumed continuous rib ligament across the enlarged hole.

## Proposed parts and load path

- Existing CFRP arm, saddles and P06 splice plates deliver arm reactions into the local webs. Any trimmed P06 quantities replace their original quantities once.
- Profile-following glass/epoxy cap strips connect each web position to the upper and lower skin sandwich. Their18 mm nominal spanwise width and0.6 mm nominal vertical thickness are proposals. The strips lie inside the original outer wing envelope, replacing foam locally.
- Explicit0.2 mm nominal skin-side bedding and0.2 mm rib-edge bedding layers separate the proposed adherends. These thicknesses are construction targets. The original wing skin/core adhesive layer remains represented and counted once.
- Thin face tapes and local collar doublers spread load from web faces into the skin caps and spar coupling. The solid laminate representations have sharp nominal intersections. Corner fillets, bend radius, continuous ply drape and ply termination must still be developed and qualified; the CAD solids do not imply a zero-radius manufacturable laminate.
- Fore/aft spar cheeks use proposed0.2 mm radial bedding and0.6 mm glass thickness. Local radial adhesive and collar face bonds connect their outer edges to the web pieces. No spar drilling or reduction of the original tube wall is proposed.
- Internal lower-skin doublers surround the inherited entry slots, with8 mm nominal overlap and0.6 mm nominal vertical laminate thickness. Their clearance cuts account for nearby carrier pieces. A doubler is a proposed load-spreading reinforcement, not proof that the interrupted skin shear flow has been restored.
- Foam is pocketed around modeled parts. Foam is not credited as the primary motor-to-wing attachment. The arm remains permanently bonded into this local carrier concept; motors and their clamp hardware retain the P05 service concept.

The intended load path is motor thrust/torque → CFRP arm → bonded saddles → splice plates → local webs → spar cheeks/collars and upper/lower skin caps → existing wing structure → retained P04 root/center attachments. The sharing between spar and skins is not yet solved. This revision does not qualify or redesign the P04 wing-center/fuselage joint.

## Assembly sequence

1. Make a wing/arm fixture referenced to the retained spar and motor-axis datums. Check the actual purchased spar section, laminate stock and cured skin/core geometry before cutting templates.
2. Prepare and dry-fit web segments, cheek pieces, cap strips, tapes and collar doublers. Establish the intended adhesive thickness using a controlled process; do not assume modeled clearances compensate for unknown manufactured tolerances.
3. Prepare the cured composite surfaces according to the selected adhesive/laminate supplier. Preserve the spar/arm structural fibers. The adhesive, surface preparation, cure cycle and environmental allowables remain unspecified.
4. With the wing open, install the lower-skin slot doublers and local caps. Bond the spar cheeks and web/collar interfaces in the fixture. Keep access for inspecting every lap and corner. The existing P06 arm carrier must be introduced before wing closure, not pushed through a finished wing as a complete hidden assembly.
5. Complete the web face tapes and shaped corner fillets, then the upper caps and skin closure. Confirm wet layup compaction and avoid adhesive-starved edges. Retained residual wing-joint adhesive allowance covers unmodeled fillets/installation excess; it is not a measured fillet mass.
6. Close exposed slot edges and the saddle/skin seam using the modeled local epoxy potting regions. Their geometry is a sealing proposal, not verified watertightness or environmental durability. The carrier remains permanently bonded.
7. Inspect, weigh and test the completed local subassembly before assigning a load capacity. Positive/reversed thrust, asymmetric motor cases, cyclic loading, moisture/temperature and local carbon interlaminar failure need evaluation.

## Checks and limitations

Native fixed constraints establish assembly positions, not bonded-joint strength. STEP exports contain the placed separate solids and names; FreeCAD constraints do not become SolidWorks mates. Create SolidWorks mates only if a native SolidWorks assembly is needed.

The validation outputs distinguish solid validity, overlap checks, solver recovery, per-solid STEP round-trip placement and material accounting. The original front lead gauges intersected the wing skin. P07 instead proposes four8 mm bundle envelopes running outside the arm and below the wing. They clear the modeled physical parts; their free-air starts do not verify the actual motor lead exit. Bend radius, clips/strain relief and ESC termination remain hardware/process decisions. The supplied motor-with-wire mass allocation is unchanged. Other P05 tool-access checks remain applicable, including bench installation of motor screws.

Joint demand screens retain the inherited hypothetical thrust, including asymmetric/reversed support reactions. A separate bounding case assigns the full local torsional demand to the spar cheeks with no favorable sharing from the skins. It uses the generated cheek centroid separation and a50% effective bond-area assumption. This is an average shear demand screen; it is not a carbon/adhesive allowable or a peel/fatigue qualification. Updated P06 plate/bond screens must use the revised geometry rather than inherited pass labels.

**The radial spar-to-rib edge-transfer screen fails:** approximately12.255 MPa demand versus the assumed2 MPa screen in the all-torque-to-spar case. The long spar/cheek bond alone is not the limiting interface. A larger developed face flange/molded connection or a substantiated alternative load path must replace/resolve that edge transfer before release. An alternative all-torque-to-skin-caps case has lower average demand but does not establish real load sharing.

The full initial pair check is retained; final slot changes are valid subtractive reliefs. All prior affected clash families were rechecked, and revised wire bundles were checked against all physical parts. Fifteen nominal wet-bond intersections below0.02 mm³ remain documented as construction-layer ambiguity; no rigid-part clash remains. Foam compatibility uses explicit pocket construction and removed-volume conservation, with the full compound/core Boolean deferred. Thin skin/bond checks cover the outer bedding interfaces. The final fit report records these limits rather than claiming a whole-aircraft interference qualification.

The8 mm slot-patch blank expansion is trimmed by the local contour window: actual bounding-outline extensions are6.1 mm forward of spar slots and5.7 mm aft of rear slots. Reliefs interrupt that perimeter, so these are not verified continuous bonded-lap widths. A final patch/ply layout and local skin shear-flow assessment remain required.

No new global wing stiffness is credited. The previous reduced-stiffness concern remains. Native skin Boolean volume conservation remains unresolved, so no skin cutout mass credit is accepted. The existing motor safe screw-depth uncertainty and21 V motor versus6S battery incompatibility also remain unchanged.

## Mass rules

New glass and explicit adhesive solids are integrated at assumed densities. Modified ribs, splice pieces and foam replace their earlier rows once. The retained wing skins, spars and original wing bond layers are replacement/context exports, not additional aircraft mass. New net explicit adhesive is moved from the remaining P06 wing saddle/rib adhesive allocation before any increase is counted. The200 g reserve remains separate and unchanged. The previously removed generic local-hardware and embedded motor-mount allowances are not restored.

## Construction reference

[WEST SYSTEM bonding and clamping](https://www.westsystem.com/instruction/epoxy-basics/bonding-and-clamping/) describes surface preparation, bonding area, fillets and fiberglass reinforcement over fillets. It supports the construction approach only; its general guidance does not qualify this aircraft geometry, thin laminate schedule, selected adhesive or joint capacity.
